PROOF OF THE APPROACH

When the plan changes, find the path forward.

PROJECT STORY 01

PRIORITIZATION × RESOURCE MANAGEMENT

Customer Stories

A significant timeline change turned a staggered webpage rollout into a 4–6 week push to complete priority stories ahead of an in-person event.

THE CHALLENGE

The project included ten customer stories with webpage assets that needed to be updated. Only three had been completed or were already in progress when the timeline shifted significantly.

THE APPROACH

I pulled together resources and met with stakeholders to reassess the workload, identify where additional support could be brought in, and determine which lower priority work could be adjusted.

THE OUTCOME

We prioritized the most important and newest stories and completed the priority work needed for the in-person event — the project's most immediate goal.

What it reinforced

When timelines change, the answer isn't simply to move faster. Reassess priorities, resources, and tradeoffs — then make the path forward clear to everyone involved.

PROJECT STORY 02

AUTONOMY × PROBLEM SOLVING

50+ Course Guides

Early in a large course-guide project, I encountered a roadblock while the manager teaching me the process was out of the office.

THE CHALLENGE

I was still learning how this particular project type worked and couldn't rely on the person who had been walking me through the process.

THE APPROACH

Instead of waiting, I reviewed previous projects for notes, comments, and progression history. I also identified team members who may have worked on similar projects and reached out for additional context.

THE OUTCOME

I pieced together the information from those sources, developed a solution, and was able to continue moving the project forward.

What it reinforced

Autonomy doesn't mean having every answer. It means knowing how to find information, use the resources around you, and avoid letting uncertainty become a blocker.

MY APPROACH TO PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Clarity before complexity.

01

Understand before acting.

Start with the objective, stakeholders, requirements, constraints, and definition of success.

02

Make the work visible.

Clear owners, deadlines, dependencies, and status make it easier for everyone to understand what needs to happen next.

03

Surface problems early.

A potential blocker is much easier to solve when the team has time and context to respond to it.

04

Keep learning.

Every project builds context. Capture what worked, what didn't, and what could make the next project better.
